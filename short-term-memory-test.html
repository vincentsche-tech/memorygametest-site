🧠 What Is Short-Term Memory?

The brain's notepad

Short-term memory is the system that holds a small amount of information for a few seconds to a minute — the phone number you just looked up, the name someone told you, the items on your mental shopping list. Psychologists often place it in the Atkinson-Shiffrin model as the stage between sensory input and long-term storage.

How much can it hold?

Classic research suggests a capacity of about 7 ± 2 "chunks" of information. A chunk can be a single digit or a grouped pattern — which is why 4-8-2-9-1-3 is easier to hold when you read it as 482-913. Modern estimates put the true limit closer to 3-5 chunks, but the everyday takeaway is the same: short-term memory is small and easily overloaded.

How long does it last?

Without active rehearsal, most short-term memories fade within 20-30 seconds. That's why a number slips away if you get distracted before dialing it. Repeating it (subvocal rehearsal) keeps it alive a little longer — until it's either used or lost.

📊 What Your Short-Term Memory Score Means

A typical range

Reaching 5-9 digits is average for adults. If you consistently recall 9+, your short-term (and working) memory is above average. A lower span on a tired or distracted day is completely normal — it reflects your state, not your ability.

Scores are personal baselines, not labels

Treat the number as a snapshot of today's focus, not a fixed trait. Track your best over weeks and you'll see whether your recall is trending up, holding steady, or dipping — useful feedback about sleep, stress, and attention.

💡 How to Improve Your Short-Term Memory

Use chunking

Group information into meaningful units. A 10-digit number becomes three chunks when split by area code and suffix. Chunking is the single most reliable way to stretch what your short-term memory can hold.

Rehearse and reduce distraction

Silently repeating new information keeps it active longer. Equally important: cut interruptions. A buzzing phone or a half-listened conversation shreds short-term memory, because the system can only hold so much at once.

Sleep and exercise

Short-term recall depends on a well-rested brain. Regular sleep, hydration, and aerobic exercise all support the attention and encoding that short-term memory relies on — so the test is really a window into your daily cognitive habits.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a short term memory test?

A short term memory test measures how many items — usually digits or words — you can hold and recall after a brief delay. Our digit-span version flashes a number sequence and asks you to repeat it, adding a digit each round until you reach your limit.

How many digits is a normal short-term memory?

Most adults recall 5-9 digits in a row. Reaching 9+ suggests above-average short-term capacity. The number shifts with fatigue and attention, so track your best rather than a single result.

Is short-term memory the same as working memory?

They overlap but aren't identical. Short-term memory is the temporary storage; working memory also includes manipulating that information, like reversing a sequence. Our digit-span test mainly challenges storage, a core part of working memory.

Can you improve short-term memory?

You can improve your effective performance with chunking, rehearsal, and fewer distractions, and support the underlying system with sleep and exercise. The raw capacity is fairly stable, but how well you use it can absolutely get better.

How long does short-term memory last?

Typically 20-30 seconds without rehearsal. Repeating information keeps it active; otherwise it decays or gets bumped out by new input. That's why a fact on the tip of your tongue vanishes if you're interrupted.
